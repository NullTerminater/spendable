## Decisions (answered by the owner on 2026-09-14)

Answers to the questions in section 1 below, applied throughout the build:

1. **Signing:** free Personal Team `UW2KV7XB66`, automatic signing; `xcodebuild -allowProvisioningUpdates` approved. App Group id `UW2KV7XB66.spendable` (Team-ID style, no portal registration). File-based login keychain. Widget sandboxed; main app not.
2. **Repo:** `~/Developer/spendable`, outside any other git checkout (the builder's choice after the owner deferred; `~/personal` is the working tree of a public repo). 3. **Credit cards and bills:** a card's entered statement balance is subtracted as one obligation per cycle. Subscriptions billed to a card are counted through the card, never twice. Scheduled payments to merchants (a Verizon autopay, rent, utilities) are ordinary recurring bills and ARE subtracted. Only payments to the owner's own accounts (credit-card payments, transfers to own savings) are classified as transfers; the word AUTOPAY alone never makes a charge a transfer.
4. **Silent changes:** bills auto-confirmed at three occurrences and keyword-backed account-type guesses count immediately, visibly badged and reversible; accounts with no keyword match are held out until typed.
5. **Annual charges:** manual bill entry in milestone 2 and the single-occurrence "looks like a yearly charge" heuristic in milestone 5.
6. **Stale and dead accounts:** the two-tier rule (stale stays in the sum marked "as of", dead drops out and is named under the number). The menu bar shows the number with a warning glyph.
7. **Balance trend:** snapshots are collected; no trend UI in v1.
8. **Open at login:** toggle, default on after the first successful connection.
9. **Generated project:** the `.xcodeproj` is not committed; `scripts/bootstrap.sh` regenerates it from `project.yml`.
10. **Sandbox:** main app not sandboxed.

**Additional rule from the owner:** a negative safe-to-spend is never shown as a negative headline. It reads "Safe to spend: $0 (balance: −$120)" — the headline is $0 and the true figure follows in parentheses. The until-payday figure follows the same rule and its per-day allowance becomes $0. The disclosure still explains the shortfall in a sentence ("You're $120 short of this month's bills"). Wherever the text below says a negative figure is "never clamped", this rule wins.

- The protocol document is v2 (changelog dated 2026-03-19). Without a `version` parameter the server returns the v1 shape your spec describes (`errors` as strings, `org` per account). With `version=2` it returns `errlist` (structured: `code`, `msg`, optional `conn_id` / `account_id`), `connections[]`, and `conn_id` per account. The two shapes are mutually exclusive on the wire. v1 is deprecated. The plan builds against v2, which also makes "show each error next to the affected account" possible.
- Transactions carry `id`, `posted`, `amount`, `description`, `payee`, `memo`, `transacted_at`, and `mcc`. `posted` may be `0` while pending. Transaction ids are unique only within an account; the demo data reuses every id across its three accounts, so the unique index is `(account, id)`.
- **Every `/accounts` request is capped at a 90-day range.** A year-back request is silently trimmed to the most recent ~89 days with a `gen.api` warning. Ranges over 45 days already get a "may be capped in the future" warning. History depth varies per institution (typically 30–90 days). The spec's one-request year of history is not possible; first sync is chunked.
- **Quota: 24 `/accounts` requests per day**, warnings first, then the access token is disabled, which forces a fresh setup token by hand. The app keeps a local daily budget.
- The reusable `claim/demo` token on the protocol page is dead (403). The developer guide at `beta-bridge.simplefin.org/info/developers` serves a fresh single-use `DEMO-v2-...` setup token on every page load, and the access URL it yields is `demo:demo` public data. Demo data: three accounts, ~340 transactions per 90 days, no credit card, no pending rows, no errors, nothing subscription-shaped. Synthetic fixtures are required for those cases.
- The claim `POST` with `Content-Length: 0` and the 403 meaning are confirmed; the protocol adds that a 403 on claim may mean the token was compromised and should be disabled on the SimpleFIN site.
- GRDB latest is 7.11.1 (2026-06-18), Swift 6 strict-concurrency ready.

## 2. Decisions I will make by default (veto at any milestone review)

These came out of the audit as facts or as choices with one sensible answer. Listed so nothing is decided silently.

- **SimpleFIN protocol version and response shape (spec lines 55–59, 87 describe the v1 shape while asking for version=2).** v2 only: send version=2, decode {errlist[{code,msg,conn_id?,account_id?}], connections[{conn_id,name,org_id,org_name,org_url,sfin_url}], accounts[{…,conn_id}]}; institution name = connections[].name (fallback org_name) joined on conn_id; tolerate legacy errors[]/org as a fallback; ignore /info (still reports 1.0). Route errors by prefix: act.* → account row, con.* → every account with that conn_id, gen.auth → global banner + re-claim prompt, gen.api → log only. HTTP 402 → 'your SimpleFIN subscription needs renewing'. Claim 403 wording includes the protocol's compromise warning ('if you didn't use it elsewhere, someone else may have — disable it on the SimpleFIN site'). *(Verified live by six lenses; v1 is deprecated with a sunset clock and cannot attribute errors to accounts, which line 59 requires. Nothing here depends on the user's preference.)*
- **Transaction and account identity.** UNIQUE(account_row_id, sfin_transaction_id) on transactions; account natural key (source, conn_id, external_id); internal INTEGER primary keys everywhere; store mcc/payee/memo/transacted_at as optional columns; effective_date = posted if >0 else transacted_at else first_seen_at; index on (account_id, effective_date DESC). *(The protocol says ids are unique only within an account and the demo reuses every id across accounts; a global unique index (line 160) would silently drop one account's history. A fact, not a choice.)*
- **Money type (line 57 Int64 cents vs line 198 'Decimal amounts').** Int64 cents end to end; milestone 3's item reads 'numeric-string to Int64-cents parsing' (exact parse, leading zeros and 0–2 fractional digits accepted, more rejected); Decimal only inside the currency formatter at the display edge. Headline figures in bar/widget/window are whole dollars floored toward negative infinity; disclosure, balances and rows show exact cents; annual→monthly = cents/12 rounded half-up; Locale.current (en_US on this Mac) with currency code pinned to USD. *(The spec's own rule is explicit and argued; the milestone wording is a slip. Formatting is craft, not preference.)*
- **Request budget and sync cadence (24 requests/day Bridge quota, token disabled on overrun).** One NSBackgroundActivityScheduler: balances-only every 6 h (Settings offers 4/6/12 h), tolerance 1 h, random fixed minute; full transaction pull once per local day; launch poll only if last success >6 h old; manual refresh allowed while a local daily budget of 12 remains, else 'Already refreshed today — SimpleFIN only gets new bank data about once a day'; on wake or day change, one debounced balances-only sync if overdue; gen.api quota warnings back off for the day and go to a diagnostics pane. *(Bounded by an external quota; any answer inside the budget looks the same to the user. Overrun forces the manual re-claim the spec is designed to avoid, so the builder must own this.)*

Use available-balance only for accounts whose type is confirmed checking; flag as suspicious if available-balance > balance on a checking account. *(The protocol does not define liability sign and real feeds vary by institution; the user cannot know what their bank reports, so a per-account override is the only correct design regardless of what they say.)*
- **Meaning of 'unpaid', overdue bills, pending matches, per-day allowance edge cases.** A confirmed charge expected anywhere in the current month stays subtracted until matched, dismissed or cancelled; overdue ones read 'expected Sep 5 — hasn't shown up yet'. A match = settled or pending transaction at the same normalized merchant, amount within ±5%, dated within ±5 days; a pending match counts as paid only when available-balance is the figure in use; the disclosure states which rule applied. Weekly/biweekly charges are expanded per occurrence across the window, including across the month boundary for the until-payday figure. Per-day allowance: days from today inclusive to the next payday exclusive, min 1; a payday today counts as already deposited; no weekend shifting; integer division floors. 'Maybe cancelled?' is measured against the paying account's balance-date, not wall-clock, and flagged charges leave the totals. *(These are arithmetic-consistency rules the user (self-described financially illiterate) would not have a preference on; the conservative reading is implied by the spec's own word 'unpaid'.)*
- **Detection rules: ±5% band vs price increases, biweekly cadence, dismissal memory, normalization, fixtures.** Band = ±5% of the series' current amount; a sequential charge within −25%/+50% arriving ≥0.75× interval after the last one continues the series as a price change ('went up from $9.99 to $10.99 in Aug'); an overlapping one creates a second series at the same merchant. Add biweekly to cadence (×26/12). Dismiss persists forever keyed by merchant+cadence+amount band with Undo in Settings. Candidates are settled, negative amounts only. Normalization rule set and synthetic fixture list per the subscription lens; captured demo JSON (already in the scratchpad: v2.json/B–E.json) is the milestone-3 parsing fixture; line 34 means 'no real data', so synthetic fixtures are allowed. *(The spec's own test list (line 128) requires the price-increase case to be handled; the rest is builder craft with no user-visible fork.)*

The user cares that line 173 holds, not how; there is only one way to satisfy it with zero dependencies.)*

App calls WidgetCenter.reloadTimelines(ofKind:) after every blob write; widget timeline uses .after(next local midnight) with pre-generated day-boundary wording entries instead of 15-minute polling; widget target links no GRDB. *(WidgetKit's 40–70 reload/day budget and lines 155/175 make the 15-minute cadence self-contradictory; the reload-after-write pattern is strictly better for 'reflect my own edits' and has no user-visible tradeoff.)*

**Scope**

### Milestone 3 — SimpleFIN client (v2), Keychain persistence, chunked and budgeted sync, memory test

**Scope**

**Scope**
